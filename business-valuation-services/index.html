Clear Definition

What Is a Business Valuation?

A business valuation is an independent, professional estimate of what your company is worth, expressed as its fair market value — the price a willing buyer and a willing seller would agree on, both reasonably informed and neither under pressure to act. A certified valuation goes beyond a rule-of-thumb estimate: it documents the methods, data, and assumptions behind the number so it holds up with the IRS, a court, a lender, or a buyer.

Whether you are exploring a sale, structuring a partnership agreement, or satisfying compliance requirements, knowing your business's true economic value provides the baseline for sound strategic decisions.

Methodology & Rigor

How We Value Your Business (Our Approach)

There is no single formula for value. We weigh three recognized approaches and apply the ones that fit your business, your industry, and the reason for the valuation:

01

Income Approach

Values your business based on its earning capacity and projected cash flows. We use Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) modeling and Capitalized Cash Flow methods to reflect your true operational earnings power.

02

Market Approach

Compares your business to real transaction sales of similar private companies and public-company multiples, including EBITDA multiples, revenue multiples, and industry benchmarks.

03

Asset Approach

Builds value from the adjusted net worth of what the business owns and owes, restating tangible and intangible assets and liabilities to current fair market value.

Clear Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my business worth? +

Your business is worth its fair market value — the price a willing buyer and willing seller would agree on, both informed and neither forced to act. The only way to know that number with confidence is a professional valuation that examines your earnings, assets, industry, and comparable sales. A rule-of-thumb multiple can give you a rough idea, but it will not hold up with the IRS, a court, or a serious buyer.

How is a business valued? +

A certified valuation weighs up to three recognized approaches: the income approach (based on earning capacity and cash flow), the market approach (based on sales of comparable businesses and public-company multiples), and the asset approach (based on net asset value). We apply the approaches that fit your business and the purpose of the valuation, then document every assumption so the conclusion is defensible.

What is fair market value? +

Fair market value is the price at which a business would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, both reasonably informed and neither under compulsion to act. It is the standard of value the IRS recognizes for tax purposes under Revenue Ruling 59-60, and it is the basis for most valuations used in estate, gift, and 409A work.

What is the difference between a business valuation and an appraisal? +

In practice, the terms are used interchangeably for businesses — both describe a professional opinion of value. The distinction that matters is who prepares it. A certified business valuation from an ABV- or ASA-accredited analyst follows recognized professional standards and documents its methods, which is what makes it defensible to the IRS, a court, or a lender.

Do I need a certified valuation analyst? +

For any valuation that will be reviewed by a third party — the IRS, a court, a lender, or the other side of a transaction — yes. Certifications such as ABV and ASA signal that the valuation follows recognized professional standards. For high-stakes purposes like estate and gift tax, 409A, ESOP, and litigation, a certified analyst is effectively required for the number to stand up.

How much does a business valuation cost? +

A standard business valuation typically ranges from $7,500 to $25,000, quoted as a fixed fee up front so there are no hourly surprises. Narrower engagements such as a 409A valuation generally run $2,500 to $9,000. The exact fee depends on the size and complexity of your business and the purpose of the valuation. Request a fixed-fee estimate and we will give you a firm number before any work begins.

How long does a business valuation take? +

Most standard valuations are complete in 4 to 8 weeks from the point we receive your documents, and narrower engagements such as a 409A are often done in 2 to 4 weeks. The main driver of the timeline is how quickly we receive complete financial information, so we provide a clear document checklist up front. Rush engagements are available when you are working against a deadline.

Can I value my own business myself? +

You can estimate it, but a self-prepared number will not carry weight with the IRS, a court, a lender, or a buyer, who all expect an independent, certified opinion. An outside valuation also removes the appearance of bias, which matters most in exactly the situations where value is contested — sales, disputes, and tax filings.
